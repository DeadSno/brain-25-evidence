📐 Methodology and data limitations

Sources

  • PubMed E-utilities — number of publications by year 2015–2025 for 130 supplements
  • Wikipedia REST API — monthly ru.wikipedia views (90 of 130 supplements)
  • OpenAlex — citations by year (130 of 130 supplements, not used in the scatter plot)

Growth formula

growth_pub  = pubmed[id][2025] / pubmed[id][2015]
growth_wiki = avg(wiki[id][last 6 months]) / avg(wiki[id][first 6 months])

Assumptions

  • Growth > ×1.5 — “significant”, > ×2.0 — “anomalous”
  • Z-score > 2σ — statistical outlier (anomaly)
  • For wiki we take the first/last 6 months — this smooths seasonality

Limitations

  • PubMed has indexed preprints since 2019 — part of the 2020–2022 growth may be an artefact
  • Wiki views ≠ supplement consumption, they show interest (sometimes driven by news events)
  • OpenAlex is incomplete before 2023 — year-over-year citation comparison is unreliable
  • The catalog has 130 supplements; series were collected for 130 (PubMed and citations) and 90 (Wiki). The “Sport” (2) and “Sleep” (3) categories are small, so their conclusions are less stable than for “Cognition” (26)

Series coverage

PubMed publication and citation series were collected for all 130 catalog supplements — before the DATA-P1-12 data collection this was 94 of 130. A Wikipedia view series exists for 90 of 130: the other 40 have no ru.wikipedia article with view history, so the second metric is not computed for them. The “Highlights” section and the z-score computation include 86 supplements — only those with both metrics. Four more (Cranberry, D-mannose, Yohimbine, Shatavari) have no non-zero value for 2015 or 2025 in the PubMed series, and without both yearly values growth is not computed.

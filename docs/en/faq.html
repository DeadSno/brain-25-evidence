Frequently asked questions

1. What is a meta-analysis?

A meta-analysis is when researchers pool 10–50 individual studies and compute an average effect. It is more reliable than a single study: random errors cancel out, systematic ones become visible. We base verdicts on meta-analyses, not on individual RCTs.

2. What is Hedges' g?

Effect size in standard deviations: how far the experimental group differs from the control. g=0.2 = small effect, 0.5 = medium, 0.8+ = large. g<0 = works in reverse (harm or opposite effect). We take g from meta-analysis abstracts, check it against the prior, and record only verified values.

3. What is an A–D grade?

A = firmly proven (g≥0.4, volume≥100)
B = moderately proven (g 0.2–0.4, volume≥50)
C = heavily studied, small effect (volume≥100, g<0.2)
D = little data (volume<50)
The grade is recalculated automatically from g and scienceIndex.

4. Why do some supplements “work” with scienceIndex < 100?

The verdict is assigned manually following the methodology: study quality, consistency of results, biological plausibility. scienceIndex = volume of science, not quality. Ginger works with an index <100 because the MAs agree and the mechanism is clear.

5. How to read the evidence quadrant?

X = Hedges' g (effect size), Y = scienceIndex (volume of science, log scale). Bubble size = metaCount (number of MAs), colour = verdict.
A big bubble high on the right = lots of science + strong effect (creatine).
A big bubble high on the left = lots of science + weak effect (glucosamine).
Supplements without g are marked “awaiting verification” — we have no data, it is not an invention.

6. What if the price is not found?

Click “suggest” — a GitHub issue with the supplement name opens. We add the price manually. An honest null is better than an invented number.

7. Can I take everything at once?

See the “Interactions” tab on the card. Critical pairs (St John's wort+SSRI, 5-HTP+tramadol) are life-threatening. Antagonists (calcium+iron) — space them 2–4 hours apart. Synergists (magnesium+B6) — can be taken together.

8. When should I expect an effect?

See the “When will I feel it” block on the card. Creatine = 2–4 weeks, melatonin = 30–60 minutes, omega-3 = 8–12 weeks. If no effect is listed = no data, or the effect is not felt subjectively (track it with a blood test).

Audit v: frequent questions

What do the grades A / B / C / D mean?

A — works very well: multiple Cochrane reviews and large RCTs (Creatine, Vitamin D, Iron). B — works: the effect is confirmed by systematic reviews (Omega-3, Zinc, Melatonin). C — depends on context: the effect exists only under certain conditions (dose, population) — Resveratrol, NMN. D — not confirmed: no systematic data or no effect found — Biotin, Yohimbine, Tribulus.

How exactly did you check the supplements?

Using an adapted PRISMA methodology (5 of 27 items): search for systematic reviews and meta-analyses in PubMed → screening by relevance → deduplication → manual reading of the top-15 abstracts → assigning a grade and verdict. All 130 audit cards are openly available in data/processed/audit/ with the PMIDs of the key sources.

Why does a popular supplement have grade D?

Popularity ≠ evidence. Biotin “for hair and nails” has no systematic review with a positive outcome in healthy people. Yohimbine was studied in the 1980s–90s and research stopped after Viagra appeared. Grade D does not mean “bad supplement”, it means “no evidence”.

Can the verdicts be trusted completely?

Verdicts are a manual assessment based on systematic review abstracts, not a clinical recommendation. Limitations: we read abstracts, not full texts; some reviews are themselves of low quality. For decisions about taking supplements, consult a doctor. The project gives no medical advice — only data.

🧮 Supplement calculator

How does the calculator work?

You pick supplements → set weight, sex, age, condition → the calculator shows: dosages (weight_based ones are recalculated by weight), drug conflicts, synergies from 7 verified pairs, warnings (iron for men, 60+, pregnancy).

Can I save my stack?

Yes. The stack is saved in localStorage (browser), and there is a “Copy link” button — a URL like ?list=Креатин:5,Омега-3:2 restores the list in any browser.

Where do the dosages and conflicts come from?

Dosages come from dosage_parsed.json (94 of 130 cards; for the rest — a fallback based on the norm). Conflicts come from the field interactions in data.json (130 cards, 199 pairs in total, with severity).

Is this medical advice?

No. The calculator shows dosages from studies and known conflicts, but it does not account for your medical history, medications or test results. For decisions — see a doctor.

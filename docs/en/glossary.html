Evidence-based medicine terms

Methods and statistics

How a supplement's effect is assessed at all, and what the numbers in the table mean.

TermDefinition
Meta-analysisStatistical pooling of 10–50 studies to get an average effect
RCTsRandomized controlled trial: the gold standard of evidence
Hedges' gEffect size in standard deviations (0.2=small, 0.5=medium, 0.8+=large)
CI (confidence interval)The range in which the true effect lies with 95% probability

Project metrics

The scales this site uses instead of the word “works”.

TermDefinition
Science IndexRCTs + 5×meta-analyses: the volume of science per supplement
GradeA–D: firmly proven / moderately / lots of science, little effect / little data
VerdictWorks / depends on context / not confirmed / insufficient data

Properties and interactions

How supplements differ from each other and how they combine.

TermDefinition
BioavailabilityThe percentage of a substance that reaches the blood (depends on the form)
SynergistA supplement that enhances another's effect (Omega-3 + Vitamin D)
AntagonistA supplement competing for absorption (calcium+iron)
UL (upper limit)Upper safe limit of the daily dose
OnsetTime to onset (30 min / 2–4 weeks / not perceptible)
